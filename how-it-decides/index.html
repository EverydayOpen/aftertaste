03 · How it decides

How Aftertaste decides what belongs to an app

Every item gets a tier and a one-sentence reason. Nothing can raise a tier above the ceiling of its folder's rule, and any doubt lowers it. When the evidence is thin, the item is listed and left alone.

These rules describe the code in the repository. In development. No public release yet. The rules will change as testers find matches that are wrong; the changelog will say so.

The short version

  1. The item sits directly in a Library folder that Aftertaste knows about, or is a launch item or receipt that names the app.
  2. Its name carries the app's exact bundle ID, or a system record proves the link. A name that only looks like the app is the weakest evidence.
  3. No installed app owns it, nothing is running from it, and it is not on the never-touch list.
  4. Only items that rebuild themselves or are only settings reach High, and only High is ticked for you.

Everything else is shown with its reason: Medium may hold your data, Review may be shared, Hands off and Needs admin are never moved.

Before any rule: what is never touched

Aftertaste asks first whether an item may be touched at all. These are listed with the reason where it helps, and they have no checkbox. The list is fixed in the code; your own keep-list can only add to it.

~/Library/Mobile Documents, CloudStorage, CloudDocs, FileProvider (iCloud)
~/Library/Keychains, Mail, Messages, Safari, Photos, Accounts, AddressBook
Photos libraries, device backups (MobileSync), ~/.Trash, ~/Library/Developer
anything named com.apple.* (except Xcode, iWork and a few developer tools)
/System, /usr, /bin, /sbin, /private (receipts are shown, read-only)
anything an installed app owns or shares, anything whose app is running
a link that leads outside the Library, a locked item, a mounted drive
Aftertaste's own journal, inventory and preferences
the apps and folders on your own keep-list

How strong is the match?

  1. Exact

    The name is the bundle ID, the ID plus a known extension (.plist, .savedState), or a Team ID plus the ID. This can reach High.

    exact id

  2. Proven

    A system record names the app: a launch agent whose program sits inside the removed app, a container whose label names the ID, an installer receipt. This can reach High where the folder's rule allows it.

    proof

  3. Structural

    A helper suffix such as .helper or .updater, or a developer-ID prefix. Medium at best, because other apps from the same developer use the same names.

    medium

  4. By name

    The app's display name at a word boundary, at least five characters, and not a common word such as Notes or Music. Review at best, never ticked.

    review

There is no fuzzy matching. If nothing above applies, the item is not listed as the app's at all.

Why exact IDs first

  • People report that cleaning up one app by name also picked up files that belong to its siblings: a browser and a mail client from the same maker, or a chat app and the rest of an office suite.

    Pearcleaner#393 · closed

  • People report that a second cleaning pass still finds files the first one missed.

    Pearcleaner#312 · closed

  • One person reports that iCloud Drive slowed down right after a cleanup run, and that Apple's consistency checker then listed hundreds of broken entries. The cause is unconfirmed.

    PureMac#142 · closed

Our paraphrase of three public bug reports about other cleaning tools, as they stood on 3 October 2026. The reports are theirs and we have not reproduced them. They are why Aftertaste matches exact bundle IDs first, leaves iCloud alone and ticks only what rebuilds itself or is only settings. What an uninstall leaves, folder by folder.

Launch agents are Hands off in this version, even with proof: "Listed only. Removing the file would not stop a job that is already loaded." Only High is ticked when a scan appears. Medium needs the per-app "Include my data" control and a tick in the confirm sheet. Review moves one item at a time, each with its own confirmation.

Shared folders

Vendor folders such as Adobe, Microsoft, Google, Mozilla and JetBrains, shared updater caches, and Group Containers that several apps of one developer use can never rise above Review while another app might still need them. If one app of a suite is installed, the folder is Hands off. Items that look like they may be your only copy (wallets, vaults, backups, virtual machines, anything over 5 GB) are Review at most and never part of a bulk move.

When the app is already gone

Without the app, its Info.plist and signature are gone too, so Aftertaste keeps an inventory of what was installed and matches against that. In a scan for apps that are gone, an item reaches High only if it is a cache or a log with an exact ID name, no installed app has that ID, it has not changed for 30 days, and no drive that could hold apps is unplugged. Everything else is Medium at most. If a drive is missing, nothing is above Review.

What it does not look at

Not covered: keychain items, login and background item records, Launch Services and Spotlight entries, iCloud data, other users, backups and snapshots. Every preview and report also says how many places it could read: "Looked in 25 of 26 places. 1 protected by macOS." It says "Nothing found" only when it looked everywhere.

It got one wrong

A match that lists something you still need is a bug we want to hear about. Open the form, say which item and which app, and never paste secrets.
